*Rebellious Ink. A training agreement, not a licence for a self-employed artist.*

### Read this first: why this agreement is different (delete this box before signing)

**An apprentice is usually not self-employed in substance.** The other four agreements are licences for people who run their own business. An apprentice does not. They are being trained and supervised. They work under a named supervisor, on the Studio's programme, in the Studio's hours, with the Studio's equipment and consumables, on Clients the Studio assigns, at prices the Studio sets. They cannot send a substitute, and they have no business of their own to run. Those are the marks of an employee or, at the least, a worker. A contract that calls an apprentice self-employed is unlikely to survive a look at how the arrangement really runs (see the cover note, and the Ready Mixed Concrete, Pimlico Plumbers and Uber cases).

**So this draft does not pick self-employment for you.** It is a training agreement that makes the status decision explicit in clause 2 and Schedule item S5. The studio chooses one option, with an accountant or solicitor, and then runs payroll, holiday, insurance and pension to match.

| Option | What it means | Likely fit |
|---|---|---|
| A. Employee on an apprenticeship agreement | A statutory apprenticeship (Apprenticeships, Skills, Children and Learning Act 2009) with an approved standard and training provider. The Apprentice is an employee. | Only if the Studio's programme actually qualifies. Most studio-run programmes will not. Check. |
| B. Employee with a training programme | A contract of employment under which the Apprentice is trained. PAYE, holiday, sick pay, pension and employers' liability insurance apply. | The most realistic choice for a studio-run apprenticeship. |
| C. Worker | Some statutory rights (minimum wage, holiday) but not full employee rights. | Rare for a trainee under close supervision. Take advice. |
| D. Observer only | No pay, no client work, no tasks. Watching and learning only. | Only if it is genuinely observation. Any real work makes the person a worker, and then minimum wage is due. |
| E. Self-employed learner | The Apprentice runs a business of their own and pays for training. | Not recommended. Only after HMRC CEST, an accountant and a solicitor all support it. |

**National Minimum Wage and other points the studio must decide with advice.** No rates or figures are suggested here, on purpose.

- Which minimum wage rate applies depends on the Apprentice's age and on whether this is a qualifying apprenticeship. Check the current rates on GOV.UK on the date of signing and each April.
- Time spent in training counts as paid working time for employees and workers.
- A training fee, or repayment of training costs if the Apprentice leaves, can push pay below the minimum or be an unlawful deduction. The owner's default is no fee (clause 8). Do not add one without advice.
- Deductions for kit, uniform, registration or insurance can also reduce pay below the minimum.
- An unpaid trial that involves doing real work is a minimum wage risk.
- If the Apprentice is an employee or worker, the Studio must give a written statement of employment particulars on or before the first day, check the right to work, register for PAYE, assess pension auto-enrolment, and hold employers' liability insurance. This agreement does not replace those.
- The law on employment rights is changing (including under the Employment Rights Act 2025). Check what is in force on the start date.

**The Studio System already models this.** The Apprentice portal logs hours (verified by a mentor), a skills catalogue of prerequisites (Stage 0) and five stages, progression photos, and an owner-only junior-artist sign-off gated on a verified-hours target and the core skills. Only a mentor, supervisor or owner can sign a skill off. Clauses 4 and 5 follow that design.

**Registration.** The compliance register says every practitioner, apprentices included, should be registered with the council before working on the public. Confirm with the council whether it will register an apprentice, and when.

**Age.** The draft assumes the Apprentice is 18 or over. If the studio takes anyone under 18, take advice first: young-worker rules, risk assessments and a parent or guardian signature all come into play.

**Placeholders.** `[CAPITALS]` is information the studio must fill in. `[24]` or `[3]` is a proposed figure: keep it or change it. `[to confirm]` marks a term taken from the owner's brief. `**[STUDIO TO DECIDE WITH ADVICE]**` marks a decision the studio should not take without an accountant or solicitor.

## 2. Status

**2.1** Whether the Apprentice is an employee, a worker or self-employed depends on the facts of how they work, not on the label. The parties have discussed the question, and have taken advice where they thought it was needed, before signing. They record their decision here so that both know where they stand and so that pay, tax, holiday and insurance follow from it.

**2.2** The Apprentice's status is the option selected in Schedule item S5. Exactly one of Options A to E below applies. Delete the others before signing.

**2.3** **Option A (applies if the Schedule selects it): employee on an apprenticeship agreement.** This is an apprenticeship agreement within the Apprenticeships, Skills, Children and Learning Act 2009, under an approved apprenticeship standard with the training provider named in the Schedule. The Apprentice is an employee of the Studio. The Studio gives the Apprentice a written statement of employment particulars on or before their first day. This Agreement is part of their terms.

**2.4** **Option B (applies if the Schedule selects it): employee with a training programme.** The Apprentice is an employee of the Studio. The Studio gives the Apprentice a written statement of employment particulars on or before their first day. The Studio operates PAYE, assesses pension auto-enrolment, and holds employers' liability insurance. This Agreement sets out the training terms and sits alongside the statement of particulars. If they disagree, the one more favourable to the Apprentice applies.

**2.5** **Option C (applies if the Schedule selects it): worker.** The Apprentice is a worker of the Studio. They are paid through payroll, and are entitled to the National Minimum Wage, statutory paid holiday and the other rights of a worker. They are not an employee.

**2.6** **Option D (applies if the Schedule selects it): observer only.** The Apprentice attends to watch and learn. They are not paid and are not asked to do any work. They do not touch Clients, handle sharps, prepare stations, clean, answer the phone or do any task that benefits the Studio. The parties agree that if the Apprentice ends up doing work, the Studio will immediately change the Apprentice's status to Option B or C and pay them from the day work began.

**2.7** **Option E (applies if the Schedule selects it): self-employed learner.** The Apprentice runs their own business and is self-employed. The parties record that an accountant and a solicitor have advised on this, and that the Studio has run HMRC's employment status check and kept the result. The Apprentice is responsible for their own tax, National Insurance, insurance and Registration. **[STUDIO TO DECIDE WITH ADVICE. Option E is not recommended for a supervised trainee.]**

**2.8** Nothing in this Agreement removes any statutory right the Apprentice has because of their status.

**2.9** The Apprentice confirms they are entitled to work or train in the United Kingdom, and will show the Studio the evidence the law requires before starting.

## 3. Purpose and the training programme

**3.1** The Studio will train the Apprentice in tattooing, piercing or both (as the Schedule says) through the Programme, to the point where the owner can decide whether to give a Junior-Artist Sign-off.

**3.2** The Programme is the Studio's syllabus as recorded in the Academy: a prerequisites stage (Stage 0) followed by five stages (Stages 1 to 5), covering infection control, health and safety, client care, design, equipment, procedures and professional practice. The Studio may update the syllabus and will tell the Apprentice of changes.

**3.3** The Apprentice commits to taking part in the Programme in good faith: attending, logging hours honestly, uploading evidence, asking questions and acting on feedback.

**3.4** The Studio does not guarantee that the Apprentice will complete the Programme, receive a Junior-Artist Sign-off, or be offered work afterwards.

## 4. Supervisor and mentor

**4.1** The Supervisor named in the Schedule is a registered practitioner who is not themselves an apprentice. They are responsible for supervising the Apprentice's practice on Clients and for the Client's safety during those sessions.

**4.2** The Mentor named in the Schedule verifies the Apprentice's hours and signs off their skills. The Mentor and the Supervisor may be the same person.

**4.3** Until the Apprentice has the Junior-Artist Sign-off, the Apprentice works on a Client only inside the Supervisor's working hours, with the Supervisor present at the level of supervision the Programme requires for that stage and task. If the Supervisor is absent, the Apprentice does no Client work.

**4.4** The Apprentice follows the Supervisor's reasonable instructions about the work and about safety.

**4.5** The Studio may change the Supervisor or Mentor on notice, and will tell the Apprentice why. The Apprentice may ask for a change, and the Studio will consider the request fairly.

## 5. Staged sign-off

**5.1** **Stage 0 first.** Before any work on the public, the Apprentice completes the Stage 0 prerequisites in the Academy. These include: Registration where the council requires it; infection prevention and control training that the council accepts; the hepatitis B vaccination course started (or a signed declination, if the Studio allows); the signed exposure control plan; and COSHH and health and safety induction.

**5.2** **Signing off skills.** Only the Mentor, the Supervisor or the owner can mark a skill as competent. The Apprentice can mark a skill as ready for review, and cannot sign off their own skills.

**5.3** **Hours.** The Apprentice logs their practice hours in the Academy. Hours count towards the target only once the Mentor has verified them.

**5.4** **Client work.** The Apprentice works on Clients only for the tasks and at the stage the Mentor has signed off. The Studio System shows the Apprentice as supervised by the Supervisor, and Clients are told. Clients may refuse to be treated by an apprentice, and the Apprentice accepts that without complaint.

**5.5** **Evidence.** The Apprentice uploads evidence and progression photographs as the Programme requires. The Mentor gives written feedback. Photographs of Clients need the Client's recorded permission.

**5.6** **Junior-Artist Sign-off.** The owner may give the Junior-Artist Sign-off once the verified-hours target in the Schedule has been met, all core skills are signed off as competent, and the owner is satisfied that the Apprentice is ready. The sign-off is a formal decision that is recorded and cannot be undone, and may carry conditions. Meeting the target does not oblige the owner to give it, but the owner will not unreasonably delay a decision.

**5.7** **No practice outside the Programme.** The Apprentice does not tattoo or pierce any person (including themselves, friends or family) except as part of the supervised Programme at the Premises. Practising unsupervised or unregistered breaks the Studio's rules and may be a criminal offence.

**5.8** **Practice materials.** The Studio supplies practice skins and materials for the Programme.

**5.9** Target dates for each stage in the Schedule are a guide. They are not deadlines. They help the Apprentice and Mentor plan.

## 6. Hours, place and time off

**6.1** The Apprentice's working pattern, place of work and any off-the-job training time are in the Schedule. The Studio may change the pattern after consulting the Apprentice and giving at least [2] weeks' notice, unless the change is a short-term one caused by the Supervisor's absence or an emergency.

**6.2** Where the Apprentice is an employee or worker, they get at least the statutory minimum paid holiday, rest breaks and rest periods. The Schedule sets the holiday year and how holiday is booked.

**6.3** If the Apprentice cannot attend, they tell the Studio Manager as early as possible, and no later than the time in the Schedule on the day. Sick pay follows the law for the Apprentice's status.

## 7. Pay and the National Minimum Wage

**7.1** **[STUDIO TO DECIDE WITH ADVICE.]** The Apprentice's pay is set out in the Schedule: hourly rate or salary, how often it is paid, and the pay day.

**7.2** The Studio pays at least the National Minimum Wage or National Living Wage that applies to the Apprentice's age and status on every pay day. Which rate applies is for the Studio to decide with advice before signing. The Schedule records the decision and the date it was checked.

**7.3** Time the Apprentice spends in training, learning and assessment during their working hours counts as working time and is paid.

**7.4** The Studio makes no deduction from pay for training, supervision, equipment, uniform, Registration or insurance, and does not ask the Apprentice to pay for them, unless the Schedule says so and the law allows it and the Apprentice's pay stays at or above the legal minimum.

**7.5** Holiday pay, sick pay, pension contributions and other benefits follow the Apprentice's status in clause 2.

**7.6** Money paid by Clients for work the Apprentice does belongs to the Studio. The Apprentice is not paid commission unless the Schedule says so. Tips are dealt with under the Employment (Allocation of Tips) Act 2023 where it applies.

**7.7** If the Schedule selects Option D (observer only), no pay is due and the Apprentice does no work, as Option D in clause 2 says.

**9.3** The Apprentice follows the Studio's dress and appearance standards for hygiene and safety (for example, clean clothes, hair tied back, covered footwear, no jewellery on hands and wrists during procedures).

## 10. Registration, insurance and health

**10.1** The Studio makes sure the Apprentice is registered with the local authority as the council requires before the Apprentice works on the public. The Schedule says who pays the fee.

**10.2** The Studio confirms in writing with its insurer, before the Apprentice's first Client session, that its public liability insurance covers the supervised Apprentice, and tells the Apprentice what is covered. If the Apprentice is an employee, the Studio holds employers' liability insurance.

**10.3** The Apprentice completes the hepatitis B vaccination course (or signs a declination, if the Studio allows it) and gives evidence in confidence. The Schedule says who pays. The Studio keeps this information confidential and shares it only as the law or the Registration requires.

**10.4** The Apprentice does not work while they have a condition that could be passed to a Client or colleague, and tells the Studio Manager so that Clients can be rebooked. They need not give medical details.

**10.5** The Studio supports the Apprentice's health, safety and wellbeing, makes reasonable adjustments for disability, and carries out risk assessments for the Apprentice's work.

## 11. Client service and conduct

**11.1** The Apprentice puts the Client's safety and comfort first. They are honest with Clients about being an apprentice.

**11.2** The Apprentice arrives on time, completes the consent, health and aftercare steps the Studio uses, checks photographic ID for every Client at every appointment, and records every session in the Studio System.

**11.3** The Apprentice never tattoos anyone under 18 (Tattooing of Minors Act 1969), and does not pierce anyone outside the Studio's piercing age policy. Parental consent does not change this for tattooing.

**11.4** The Apprentice does not drink alcohol or take illegal drugs on the Premises or before working, and does not work while impaired by alcohol, drugs or medicine. There is no smoking or vaping inside the Premises.

**11.5** The Apprentice follows the Studio's hygiene and infection-control procedures, which the council's Registration requires of everyone on the Premises, and attends induction and refreshers.

**11.6** The Apprentice treats Clients, colleagues and visitors with respect. They do not harass, bully, discriminate against or threaten anyone, and do not film or photograph people without permission.

**11.7** The Apprentice responds to messages and enquiries in the Studio System assigned to them within [24] hours, and attends booked sessions or gives at least [48] hours' notice through the Studio System. Genuine illness, bereavement or emergency are exceptions.

## 12. Studio System, client records and data protection

**12.1** The Apprentice uses only their own login, never shares it, and uses the Studio System only for the Programme and the Studio's work.

**12.2** The Studio is the controller of Client records. The Apprentice handles them only for the supervised work, on a need-to-know basis, and follows the Studio's data protection and security rules.

**12.3** The Apprentice must not copy, export, download, photograph or screenshot Client records or the client database, or use them for any other purpose.

**12.4** Health information is special category data. It is never shared in messages, social media or personal chat apps.

**12.5** The Apprentice reports any loss, breach or wrong access to the Studio at once, and in any case within [24] hours.

**12.6** The Studio is the controller of the Apprentice's own records (hours, skills, evidence, progression photographs and reviews), uses them for the Programme, and keeps them as its staff privacy notice says. The Apprentice can ask for a copy.

**17.2** The Studio, through the Supervisor, takes responsibility for supervised work that the Apprentice carried out in line with the Studio's procedures and the Supervisor's instructions. The Apprentice is responsible for a deliberate or reckless breach of those procedures.

**17.3** The Studio handles Client complaints first, and the Apprentice cooperates.

## 18. Term, probation and ending

**18.1** This Agreement starts on the start date in the Schedule. It runs for the fixed term in the Schedule, or until the Junior-Artist Sign-off (and any agreed next step under clause 19), whichever the Schedule says.

**18.2** The first [3] months are a probation period unless the Schedule says otherwise. During it, the Supervisor and Mentor review progress at least [every 4 weeks].

**18.5** On ending, the Apprentice returns keys and Studio property, their access to the Studio System ends, and the Studio gives the Apprentice a copy of their training record (hours logged and verified, stages completed and skills signed off) and a fair reference. Final pay and accrued holiday pay are paid as the law requires.

**18.6** This Agreement contains no restriction on where the Apprentice works afterwards. Any restriction would need separate advice and agreement.

## 19. After the Junior-Artist Sign-off

**19.1** Within [14] days of a Junior-Artist Sign-off, the Studio and the Apprentice meet to agree the next step. The options are: (a) the Apprentice continues under this Agreement as a junior artist on the terms in the Schedule; (b) the Apprentice moves to one of the Studio's artist agreements (chair-rent, resident commission or piercer); or (c) the parties part on good terms.

**19.2** No move happens automatically. A change from employee or worker to self-employed is not made just because the Apprentice has more skill. It needs a new written agreement, a fresh HMRC employment status check and advice, because the way the Apprentice works has to change in fact. The Studio will not ask the Apprentice to sign a self-employed agreement unless they will really be running their own business.

**20.3** If mediation does not resolve it, either party may take it to the courts of England and Wales, or to an employment tribunal where the law gives that right.

**20.4** Nothing in this clause limits any statutory right, including to bring a claim in an employment tribunal, or limits the need for ACAS early conciliation before one.
