## 1. Purpose and the England position

This policy sets out who we will tattoo and pierce, how we check, what we ask clients to read and sign, and how we keep and protect records. It applies in England. Where the law differs in Wales or Scotland, the England position applies to Rebellious Ink.

## 2. The legal position in brief

**Tattooing.** Section 1 of the Tattooing of Minors Act 1969 makes it an offence to tattoo a person under 18, except for medical reasons by a qualified medical practitioner. It is a defence to show reasonable cause to believe the person was 18 or over and that you actually believed it. Consent from a parent or from the young person is not a defence. The studio does not rely on the defence: we check proof of age every time.

**Piercing.** There is no English statute that sets a minimum age for cosmetic piercing that we found. In Wales, sections 95 and 96 of the Public Health (Wales) Act 2017 make it an offence to perform an intimate piercing on a person under 18, and to arrange one, and neither the young person nor a parent can consent. That law does not apply in Birmingham. The studio nevertheless sets its own rule that applies to every piercing, as set out below. The studio policy is not a legal requirement in England, but we follow it without exception.

## 3. Studio age policy

| Treatment | Minimum age | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| Tattooing | 18 | Photographic ID checked at every appointment. No exceptions. No parental consent route. |
| Intimate piercing | 18 | Photographic ID checked. No exceptions, and parents cannot consent. |
| Other body piercing (other than ear or nose) | 18 | Photographic ID checked. |
| Ear piercing | 16 to 17 | Parent or guardian present in person, signs consent, and shows photographic ID. Client shows ID or other proof of age. |
| Nose piercing | 16 to 17 | As for ear piercing. |
| Any piercing | Under 16 | Not offered by studio policy. |

For this policy, intimate piercing means a piercing of the anus, breast (including nipple and areola), buttock, natal cleft, penis (including foreskin), perineum, pubic mound, scrotum, tongue or vulva. This is the list that Welsh law uses. We use it because it is the only statutory list available, and we include the tongue.

A parent or guardian who accompanies a 16 or 17 year old for an ear or nose piercing must stay for the whole appointment. We do not accept consent by telephone, message, email, a signed letter or a form sent with the young person. A parent's consent for an under-18 does not make a tattoo or intimate piercing acceptable under any circumstances.

The studio may refuse any client at any time if the artist or piercer believes the client is under age, does not understand the procedure, or is under pressure from someone else.

## 4. Age and identity checks

Every client is asked to show original photographic ID at the start of every appointment, including returning clients and clients we know. Accepted ID is a valid passport, a UK or EU photocard driving licence, a national identity card, or a PASS-accredited proof-of-age card [confirm accepted list]. Photocopies, photos on a phone, and expired documents are not accepted.

The member of staff checks that: the photograph looks like the client; the date of birth shows the client is at least 18 (or at least 16 for ear and nose piercing); the document is in date; and it does not appear altered. If any doubt remains, the appointment does not go ahead. The client is told politely, and the deposit position is as set out in section 12.

For 16 and 17 year olds, the parent or guardian shows their ID and signs to confirm they are the parent or legal guardian. Where the adult is not the parent, they show written evidence of their authority, such as a court order.

We do not photocopy or scan ID. We record only the type of document, the last four characters of its number [or "checked" if the studio decides not to record the number], the client's date of birth, and the initials of the person who checked it, on the consent form. This keeps the personal information we hold to the minimum we need.

## 5. Consent form

Every client signs a consent form on the day of treatment, before the treatment starts, after reading it. The form is on paper or on an electronic device approved by the studio, and is a copy of the template at Appendix 09 of the CIEH toolkit, adapted. It includes:

- The client's name, date of birth, address, telephone and email, and emergency contact.
- Date, treatment, body area, artist or piercer, design or jewellery chosen, and the price and deposit.
- ID check: type of ID, who checked, date.
- A statement that the client has been told what the procedure involves, that it is permanent (for tattooing) or can leave a mark or scar (for piercing), the likely healing time, the risks (such as infection, allergic reaction, scarring, keloid, bleeding, migration or rejection of a piercing, and fading or spreading of a tattoo), and the aftercare they must follow.
- A statement that the client has read the aftercare sheet (document 07) and received a copy.
- A statement that the client has had time to think, has not been pressured, and may change their mind and stop at any time before or during the procedure.
- A statement that the studio does not diagnose, and that the client has answered the medical screening questions honestly.
- The client's agreement to the privacy notice and how their records are kept (section 9).
- A record of the batch details of ink or jewellery used (document 04).
- Signature of client, signature of artist or piercer, and date and time.

## 6. Medical screening

The client answers a short set of questions on the form, and the artist or piercer discusses them in private. The questions ask, in plain words, whether the client:

- has a skin condition in or near the area (for example eczema, psoriasis, dermatitis, sunburn, rash or a cut);
- has any known allergy, to latex, nickel, metals, dyes, antiseptics or ointments;
- has a bleeding or clotting condition, or takes blood-thinning medication;
- has diabetes, a heart condition, epilepsy, a history of fainting, a condition that affects healing or the immune system, or a history of keloid or raised scarring;
- has a condition they have been told can be passed on through blood (the client does not have to give details; we use standard precautions with every client);
- is or may be pregnant or breastfeeding;
- is taking, or has recently finished, any medication that affects the skin or healing, for example some acne treatments or steroids;
- has had a tattoo or piercing before and if they had any problem;
- has eaten, and is feeling well today;
- has used alcohol or drugs in the last 24 hours.

We use the answers to decide whether it is safe to go ahead or to suggest the client speaks to their GP first. We do not diagnose or give medical advice. If an answer suggests a risk, the artist or piercer says so, and may postpone or refuse the treatment. The client may be asked to bring written confirmation from their GP, but that does not oblige the studio to proceed.

### Reasons we postpone or refuse

The artist or piercer will not go ahead if the skin in the area is broken, sunburned, infected, inflamed, or has a rash or a mole or other lesion that has not been checked by a doctor. They will not go ahead if the client has a medical condition or medication that the studio is not comfortable with, if the client seems unwell, or if the area is not suitable for the design or jewellery requested. They will not go ahead if the client seems intoxicated.

## 7. Intoxication

Treatment is refused if the client appears to be under the influence of alcohol or drugs, or says they have recently had either. We do not tattoo or pierce someone who cannot give clear, informed consent. The client may rebook for another day. The reason is recorded on the form, and the deposit position is as in section 12.

## 8. Photography consent

We photograph finished work only with the client's separate written consent. The photography section of the consent form allows the client to choose: no photographs; photographs for the client's own record and aftercare only; photographs for the studio portfolio and website without identifying the client; photographs for social media. Faces and identifying marks are not shown unless the client specifically agrees. Clients may withdraw consent for future use at any time by emailing thedesk@rebelliousink.tattoo, and we will remove photos from our website and our own social media accounts within [28] days, though we cannot control copies already shared by others. Photographs of intimate areas are never published. We do not photograph under-18 piercings for any promotional purpose.

## 9. Record keeping and UK GDPR

**What we hold.** For each client we hold the consent form, medical screening answers, ID check details, treatment details and batch records, aftercare record, photographs if consent was given, payment and deposit records, and any complaint or adverse reaction record.

**Why and on what basis.** We hold this to provide the treatment safely, to meet our registration and byelaw duties, to trace a problem back to a batch or procedure, and to deal with legal claims. Health information is special category data under UK GDPR and receives extra protection. [The studio's lawful basis and special category condition to be confirmed against ICO guidance and recorded in the studio's privacy notice. The usual approach is that the client gives explicit consent to the health questions on the form, and the studio relies on its legitimate interests and legal obligations for keeping them.]

**Retention.** The ICO says the UK GDPR does not set fixed retention periods and that we must set a period we can justify. Our period is **seven years from the date of the last treatment**. This is longer than the three-year minimum in the CIEH toolkit. It reflects the six-year time limit for most civil claims under section 2 of the Limitation Act 1980, plus one year as a margin. [Confirm the period with the insurer.] After seven years, paper records are shredded and electronic records are securely deleted, and the date of deletion is noted in a destruction log. Photographs given with consent for the portfolio are kept until the client withdraws consent or the studio stops using them, whichever is first. A client's records are kept longer only if there is a live complaint, claim or investigation.

**Where records are kept.** Paper records are stored in a locked filing cabinet in [location, on the registered premises], and the key is held by Elle Evaa and Charlie. Electronic records are held in [system name and location], protected by a strong password and two-step verification, accessible only to named staff, and backed up. Records are not kept on personal phones or personal email accounts. Staff access them only when they need to. Records are kept on the premises named in the registration so that an officer can inspect them. The council cannot ask for client information on the application, but officers may ask to see records at inspection and must be shown them while being careful about confidentiality.

**Sharing.** We do not sell or share client data for marketing. We share information only where needed to protect a person's health (for example with a medical professional after an adverse reaction, with the client's knowledge where possible), where the law requires it, or where an insurer or the council needs it for a claim or inspection.

**Rights and access requests.** A client can ask to see the information we hold about them, to have errors corrected, and, in some cases, to ask for deletion or restriction. A request may be made verbally or in writing, to thedesk@rebelliousink.tattoo. We respond within one month of receiving it and of verifying identity. We may need to keep some information to meet a legal or insurance duty, and will explain this. Elle Evaa logs each request in document 10 and decides the response with Charlie. Complaints about our data handling can be made to us first, and to the Information Commissioner's Office.

**Data breaches.** If records are lost or accessed without authority, the manager tells Charlie at once. Charlie decides whether to report the breach to the ICO within 72 hours and to inform the clients concerned. Details go in document 10.

## 10. Under-18 piercing record

For any client under 18, the record includes the parent or guardian's name, signature, ID type checked, relationship, contact details, and a note that they were present throughout. The piercer asks the young person, away from the parent, whether they want the piercing. If the young person seems unsure or seems to be under pressure, the piercing does not go ahead.

## 11. Staff responsibilities

Every artist and piercer checks ID before taking a client into the treatment area. Reception staff check ID at booking and check-in where possible. A staff member who is unsure asks Elle Evaa. No one sets aside this policy because a client is a regular, a friend, or has a deposit paid. If a person is in any doubt about age, the answer is no.
