## 1. Purpose

This policy covers the equipment used on clients and how it is made safe. It follows byelaw 3 of the model byelaws (cleansing and sterilisation of needles, instruments, jewellery, materials and equipment) and the CIEH toolkit, and should be checked against Birmingham's adopted byelaws once received.

## 2. The default: single-use and pre-sterilised

Rebellious Ink uses single-use sterile equipment as standard. This means every needle, cartridge, grip, tube, piercing needle, razor, ink cap and swab that touches a client, or that could touch blood or body fluid, is bought sterile and in sealed packaging, used for one client, and thrown away as sharps or clinical waste. Where a pre-sterilised option exists for an item, the studio buys it rather than reprocessing the item. No reusable item is sterilised on the premises unless section 4 has been completed and signed by Charlie and Elle Evaa first.

## 3. Handling single-use items

Sterile items are kept in their sealed packs in a clean, dry, closed cupboard or drawer, off the floor, away from the sink and from dust and moisture. They are used in date order, first in first out. Packs are checked before use: if a pack is open, damaged, wet, stained or past its expiry date it is thrown away as clinical waste. Staff open packs in front of the client where practical and only after hands are washed and gloves put on, or open the outer pack with clean hands and let the inner contents fall onto the sterile field.

The expiry date of every item is checked at the monthly stock check (document 11). Any pack that has been opened and not used for a client is thrown away at the end of the session. Needles and cartridges are never reused, re-sterilised, shared or cleaned and kept for later. Needle bars are single-use, or if reusable, handled under section 4.

Jewellery for piercing is sterile when supplied and in sealed packaging. Piercing jewellery is made of [materials, for example implant grade titanium and other materials that meet the supplier's declared specification]. The piercer keeps the supplier's declaration for each batch. Initial jewellery is not reused or returned. The studio does not use stock jewellery from open display on a new piercing.

Machines, power supplies, clip cords and foot pedals are covered with barrier film or covers for every client. Covers are thrown away after use. Machines that are handled by gloved hands are wiped with disinfectant at the end of the day.

## 4. If reusable items are ever used

Reusable items can include steel grips, forceps, clamps, receiving tubes, tweezers and tubes. They carry a higher risk, so they are only used where the studio has the correct equipment, a trained person, and a written record.

**Responsible person:** Elle Evaa. A named deputy is [name]. Only trained staff reprocess equipment.

**Decontamination route.** Used items are carried to the decontamination area in a closed container as soon as possible after use, and are not left to dry. The area is separate from the treatment area, [location]. The studio uses a one-way flow from dirty to clean, as in Appendix 11 of the CIEH toolkit. Operators wear heavy-duty gloves, an apron and eye protection for cleaning.

**Cleaning.** Items are cleaned thoroughly before sterilisation, because sterilisation does not work on dirt. They are rinsed in cold water, then cleaned in an ultrasonic cleaner [make and model] with the correct detergent for the time stated by the manufacturer, then rinsed with clean water and inspected under a magnifier for debris. The ultrasonic cleaner is emptied, rinsed and dried at the end of each day, and its solution is changed at least daily or when it is visibly dirty. The cleaner is tested for performance [weekly or per the manufacturer, using a recognised test such as foil or a soil test] and the results are recorded in the logbook. Items are dried, then inspected, then packed.

**Packing.** Items are placed in single-use sterilisation pouches, sealed and labelled with the date, the batch or load number and the operator's initials. Hollow or wrapped items need a steriliser of the right type (see below).

**Autoclave.** The studio uses a [make, model, serial number, class, for example Type B benchtop steam steriliser]. The CIEH toolkit explains that a Type B (vacuum) steriliser is suitable for porous, hollow and wrapped items, that a Type S is suitable only for loads it has been validated for, and that a Type N (no assisted air removal) is suitable only for solid, unwrapped items. The studio does not process hollow or wrapped items unless the autoclave is the correct type for them, and the benchtop steriliser must meet BS EN 13060. The autoclave is placed on a level, stable surface, in a clean, well-ventilated place away from the treatment area, is filled only with fresh distilled or reverse osmosis water as the manufacturer says, and is not overloaded.

**Daily tests.** Every working day before first use, the operator runs the manufacturer's start-up checks and any warm-up or air-removal test [Bowie-Dick or helix test as the manufacturer requires]. For each load, the operator checks the cycle printout or display for time, temperature and pressure, and puts a process indicator in the pouch or load. If any reading is outside limits, the load is not released: all items are re-cleaned, repacked and rerun. The daily autoclave record sheet (Appendix 14 of the toolkit, or the studio's own copy of it) is completed for every load.

**Logbook.** The logbook is kept at the autoclave and records: date; operator; load number; contents; cycle; temperature, time and pressure; indicator results; pass or fail; action taken; and weekly or monthly maintenance. Printouts are attached. The logbook is kept for at least [two years or as the insurer advises, but not less than the period in document 06]. Elle Evaa checks and signs it weekly.

**Validation and servicing.** The autoclave is validated on installation, and re-validated and serviced at least once a year by [competent engineer or company], or as the manufacturer states, and always after a repair or if it is moved. Engineer's certificates are kept with the register in document 11. The CIEH toolkit states that the Pressure Systems Safety Regulations 2000 require a written scheme of periodic examination by a competent person (pressure vessels), and that insurance cover is needed for the steriliser as a pressure vessel. The studio arranges both: written scheme held by [competent person or insurer], insurance certificate held in document 11. If the autoclave fails, it is taken out of use and clearly labelled "Do not use". Reusable items are not used on clients until a working autoclave is back and the engineer confirms it, and the studio uses single-use alternatives meanwhile.

**Storage of sterilised items.** Pouches are stored in a clean, dry, closed container, off the floor, with the date. The shelf life is [days or months in line with the pouch manufacturer's instructions]. An item whose pouch is damaged, wet or past date is reprocessed. Pouches are opened in front of the client.

**Traceability.** For each client, the consent record notes the autoclave load number (or "single-use only") so that the studio can trace which load served which client.

## 5. Ink, pigment and aftercare products

Ink must be sterile and inert to meet the byelaws. The studio buys ink only from named suppliers who provide: the product name; the batch or lot number; the manufacture date and expiry date; a list of ingredients; a safety data sheet; and a written declaration that the ink complies with the EU REACH Annex XVII Entry 75 restriction on substances in tattoo inks. This declaration is requested because Great Britain does not currently restrict the substances in tattoo ink (a UK REACH restriction was decided in December 2025 but its commencement was not confirmed when this pack was prepared), so complying with the EU standard is the practical benchmark. Charlie will review the position when the GB restriction takes effect, and update this policy [review prompt date].

Ink is stored as the manufacturer says, away from sunlight and extreme heat, with the cap closed, in a clean cupboard. Bottles are checked for damage, leaks, particles or an unusual smell. Ink past its expiry date, or showing signs of contamination, is thrown away. Ink is never diluted with tap water. Only sterile distilled water or the manufacturer's recommended diluent is used, and that diluent is also batch recorded.

Ink is poured into single-use sterile caps for each client. Leftover ink in a cap is thrown away at the end of the treatment. It is never returned to the bottle. Squeeze bottles, if used, are wiped with disinfectant, and the nozzle does not touch caps, skin or gloves. Rinse cups are single-use. Ink caps are never reused. Diluted cleaning or rinsing solutions, including any green soap solution, are made up fresh for each client in a clean labelled bottle and never topped up from a shared bottle.

**Ink batch records.** For each tattoo, the record notes: the colours used, product names and batch or lot numbers, supplier, and expiry dates. These are kept with the client record under document 06, so the studio can trace a problem to a batch and tell the supplier and the client. A batch register is kept in document 11 that lists each batch, date received, supplier declaration on file (yes or no) and date finished or discarded. If a supplier recalls a batch, that batch is removed immediately, and clients tattooed with it are told.

**Other products.** Stencil solutions, lubricants, ointments, aftercare creams and antiseptics are used in single-use sachets or dispensed without contamination. Any product that is labelled for single use is not used on a second client. Products are used in date. A numbing product, if ever requested, is handled as the toolkit says: the client buys and applies it, the studio does not supply or inject any anaesthetic, and the client reads the leaflet.

## 6. Equipment other than the above

Cleaning of non-critical equipment, such as machines handled by gloved hands, power supplies, stools, trays and lamps, follows document 03. Spray bottles for disinfectant are labelled and cleaned. Disposable razors, swabs and gauze are single-use. Ultrasonic baths and autoclaves are not used for anything other than their stated purpose.

## 7. Staff responsibilities

Anyone who handles sterile items has read this policy and has been shown the correct handling. Only those trained to operate the autoclave do so, and the training record in document 08 notes this. Any failure or doubt is reported at once to Elle Evaa. No item whose sterility is doubtful is used on a client.
