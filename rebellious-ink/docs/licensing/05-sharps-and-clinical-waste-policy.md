## 1. Purpose

This policy sets out how Rebellious Ink handles sharps and waste that may carry blood or body fluids, so that staff, cleaners, collectors and the public are not harmed. It meets the waste requirement in byelaw 2 of the model byelaws (waste handled and disposed of in accordance with relevant legislation and the local authority's advice) and the duty of care that applies to anyone who produces controlled waste under section 34 of the Environmental Protection Act 1990. Elle Evaa is responsible for day-to-day waste handling. Charlie is responsible for the contractor agreement.

## 2. Sharps

Sharps include needles, cartridges with needles attached, piercing needles, scalpel blades, disposable razors, broken glass from ink bottles that may have blood on them, and any other item that can cut or puncture skin.

A sharps bin that meets BS 7320 or UN 3291 is at every station where sharps are used, within arm's reach of the operator and below shoulder height, on a stable surface or fixed bracket, with the opening not blocked. Bins are the correct size, [litres], have a temporary closure, and are labelled with the premises name, date of assembly, and are signed. Bins are assembled by the person who first uses them, who writes the date and initials.

Needles are put straight into the sharps bin by the person who used them, immediately after use, without being passed from hand to hand, resheathed, bent, broken or removed from the cartridge by hand. If a cartridge needle cannot be separated safely, the whole item goes in. Sharps are never put in general waste or clinical waste bags, or left on trays, trolleys, or the floor.

Bins are not filled above the fill line, which is about three quarters full. Nobody pushes down on contents or reaches in. When a bin reaches the fill line it is closed using the temporary closure, then the permanent closure is locked, and the bin is labelled with the date and the premises. A full bin is moved to the clinical waste store by a trained member of staff, carried by the handle, held away from the body, never by the lid. An open bin is never carried across the premises. Bins are never stored on the floor of a treatment room, and are kept out of the reach of clients, children and visitors.

If a needle is found in the wrong place, it is picked up using tongs or forceps, never bare hands, and treated as a sharps injury risk. Staff do not recap or dispose of needles in a coffee cup or other container. A sharps injury is dealt with under document 02, section 9.

## 3. Clinical waste

Clinical waste at the studio includes anything that has been in contact with blood or body fluids, such as gloves, swabs, gauze, paper sheet if soiled, barrier film if soiled, wipes, aprons, masks and single-use gowns, ink caps and any other items. It excludes sharps, which go into a sharps bin.

Clinical waste is placed in an orange clinical waste bag in a foot-operated, lidded bin in the treatment area. The bag is not overfilled and is tied at the neck, labelled with the premises name and date, and put in the clinical waste store. Bags are never compressed by hand. Used paper from the couch that is not soiled and general waste go in black or clear general waste bags.

Items that are not visibly contaminated, such as unused sterile packaging, outer wrappers, clean paper towels and paper sheets, go in general waste. If in doubt, treat the item as clinical waste. Waste classification is confirmed with the contractor and in line with NHS guidance on the safe management of healthcare waste.

Pharmaceutical waste, expired antiseptics and chemical waste are kept separate and collected by the contractor as the safety data sheet requires. Cytotoxic and cytostatic waste is not applicable to the studio, which does not handle those medicines. If that changes, this policy is reviewed first.

## 7. Spillages, leaks and damaged bins

If a bin or bag splits or leaks, the person nearest puts on gloves and an apron, places the damaged item inside a larger sharps bin or bag, and follows the spillage procedure in document 02. Needles on the floor are picked up with tongs. If a bin is damaged or overfull in the store, it is overpacked in a larger container or closed with a spare lid and reported to Elle Evaa. The incident is logged in document 10.

## 8. Staff handling

Only trained staff move waste. Staff wear gloves and an apron when moving waste, and wash their hands afterwards. Staff are shown how to assemble, fill, close and carry bins during induction, and the training record in document 08 notes it. Cleaners and contractors who may come into contact with waste are told where it is and that sharps must never be handled.

## 9. Clients' own sharps

The studio does not accept sharps from members of the public, and clients may not put their own sharps in studio bins. Clients are not given needles or other sharps to take away.
