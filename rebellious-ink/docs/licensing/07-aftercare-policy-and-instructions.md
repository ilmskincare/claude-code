## Part 1: Aftercare policy (for staff)

### 1. Purpose

Good aftercare helps a tattoo or piercing heal well and helps clients spot problems early. This policy sets out what staff must do before the client leaves, how we follow up, and what we say when a client reports a problem. It uses the aftercare and follow-up approach in the CIEH tattooing and body piercing guidance toolkit.

### 2. Before the client leaves

The artist or piercer explains the aftercare out loud in plain English, using the written sheet below, and answers questions. The client receives a printed copy of the right sheet (tattoo or piercing) and, if they wish, a link or photo of it. The artist covers a new tattoo with a clean, suitable dressing, such as [product] and explains when and how to remove it. The piercer fits the jewellery chosen and records it, and tells the client about the likely healing time for that piercing and when it is safe to change jewellery. The client confirms on the consent form that they have received and understood the aftercare. The artist records any special advice given, for example for a larger piece, a piercing in a difficult place, or a client with a skin condition.

Staff say clearly that the studio does not diagnose. We can look at a tattoo or piercing and give general aftercare advice, but only a doctor, nurse or pharmacist can diagnose or treat a medical problem. We never recommend antibiotics, steroid creams or other medicines. We do not tell a client "it's fine" or "it's infected" about a possible problem. We tell them what to do next (section 3 below).

### 3. When a client contacts us with a concern

Reception or the artist takes the details: name, date of treatment, what has happened and when it started, and whether the client has felt unwell or has a temperature. Then:

- For normal healing questions (tenderness, flaking, itching, crusting, minor redness around the site), give the standard aftercare advice and offer to see the client for a check-in.
- If the client reports any of the warning signs listed on the client aftercare sheets in Parts 2 and 3, tell them plainly to seek medical advice from their GP, a pharmacist or NHS 111, and that if they cannot get help quickly or feel seriously unwell they should go to an urgent treatment centre or A&E. Do not delay while waiting for the studio to look.
- If there are signs of a serious allergic reaction or the client is seriously ill, tell them to call 999.
- Record the contact on the adverse reaction log in document 10 on the same day.

The studio offers a free check-in appointment [timeframe, for example within 8 weeks] for any client who wants someone to look at their tattoo or piercing. We offer a free touch-up for a tattoo [terms, for example once within 8 weeks if the client has followed the aftercare], at the artist's discretion, and not where the client has not followed the aftercare or has an infection.

### 4. Follow-up

Within [5 to 7] days of every treatment, a member of staff sends the client a brief message asking how the healing is going and reminding them how to contact us. The reply, and any advice given, are recorded on the aftercare follow-up record (Appendix 10 of the CIEH toolkit) or the client record. Staff never record medical details in a personal message app.

### 5. Under-18s

For a 16 or 17 year old having an ear or nose piercing, the aftercare is explained to the young person and to the parent or guardian, and both receive the sheet.

## Part 2: Aftercare instructions for clients: tattoo

*Hand this sheet to the client. Keep the wording simple. The studio can add the artist's own notes at the end.*

**Rebellious Ink: looking after your new tattoo**

Thank you for choosing Rebellious Ink. How you look after your tattoo in the first few weeks affects how it heals and how it looks later. Everyone heals differently, so use this as a guide and ask us if you are unsure.

**The first few hours**

Keep the dressing on for as long as your artist told you: [hours]. Wash your hands before you touch it. When you take it off, wash your tattoo gently with clean, lukewarm water and a mild, unscented soap, using clean hands only. Pat it dry with a clean paper towel. Do not rub.

**The following days and weeks**

Wash your tattoo gently once or twice a day, then pat it dry. Put on a thin layer of the aftercare product we recommended, [product], and only as much as the skin can take. Too much can clog the skin. Wear loose, clean clothing over it. Change your bedding if it gets stained or dirty.

Expect some redness, tenderness, a light weep of fluid or ink, and later some flaking, itching and peeling. This is common. Do not pick, scratch or peel your tattoo. Let any scabs and flakes come off on their own.

**Things to avoid while it heals**

Do not soak the tattoo. That means no swimming pools, hot tubs, sea water, saunas or long baths until your artist tells you it is healed. Showers are fine. Keep it out of direct sun and do not use sunbeds. Once it has healed, a high-factor sunscreen helps protect it. Avoid tight, rough or dirty clothing over it. Do not use any cream, oil or ointment that has not been recommended, and do not use a plaster or cling film unless your artist told you to. Keep pets away from it. Avoid heavy exercise that stretches or rubs the area. Do not let anyone else touch it, and always wash your hands first.

**When to get medical advice**

We are not doctors and we do not diagnose. If you are worried about your tattoo, speak to a medical professional. Contact your GP, a pharmacist, or call NHS 111 if you notice any of the following:

- redness that is spreading, or getting worse after the first few days;
- the area feeling increasingly hot, swollen or painful rather than better;
- yellow or green discharge, pus, or a bad smell;
- a fever, shivering, feeling generally unwell, or red streaks going away from the tattoo;
- a rash, blisters or lumps that do not settle;
- a lot of itching or swelling that seems like an allergic reaction.

Go to A&E or an urgent treatment centre, or call 999, if you have difficulty breathing, your face, lips, tongue or throat are swelling, you feel faint or collapse, or you feel seriously unwell. Do not wait to contact us first.

Please also let us know afterwards, by email at thedesk@rebelliousink.tattoo or by phone on [studio phone], so that we can record it and help with the aftercare. We offer a free check-in if you would like us to see it.

**Healing and touch-ups**

Most tattoos look their best when fully healed, which can take several weeks. Your artist will tell you what to expect for your design. If you think your tattoo needs a touch-up, get in touch with us [within time frame]. We do not touch up a tattoo that has not healed or that shows signs of a problem.

## Part 3: Aftercare instructions for clients: piercing

**Rebellious Ink: looking after your new piercing**

Thank you for choosing Rebellious Ink. A new piercing is a wound, so it needs careful cleaning and patience. Healing times vary a lot depending on the body area, the person, and how well it is looked after. Your piercer will tell you the usual healing time for your piercing and when you can change the jewellery.

**Cleaning**

Wash your hands with soap and water before touching your piercing, every time. Clean your piercing [once or twice a day] with sterile saline solution (such as [product]) that your piercer recommended. Spray or soak and let any crust soften, then gently pat dry with a clean disposable paper towel or gauze. Do not use cotton wool or towels, which can leave fibres in the piercing. Do not use cleansers that sting, such as alcohol, hydrogen peroxide or antiseptic creams, unless a health professional has advised it.

**Looking after it day to day**

Leave the jewellery in. Do not twist, turn, pick at or play with it, and do not remove it, because the hole can close quickly. Keep hair products, make-up, perfume and lotions away from it. Wear clean, loose clothing and sleep on clean bedding. Keep phones, headphones and hats clean if they touch the area. For ear piercings, avoid sleeping on the piercing. Avoid swimming pools, hot tubs, sea water and baths until your piercer says it is healed. Showers are fine. For mouth and tongue piercings, rinse after eating and drinking, and avoid very hot, spicy or sticky food at first. For nose piercings, do not blow your nose hard or pick at it.

Some swelling, tenderness, bruising and clear or pale fluid that dries to a crust is common. Do not change the jewellery before your piercer says it is safe. Never let anyone else change it for you except a professional piercer.

**When to get medical advice**

We are not doctors and we do not diagnose. If you are worried about your piercing, speak to a medical professional. Contact your GP, a pharmacist, or call NHS 111 if you notice any of the following:

- redness that is spreading, or swelling and pain that are getting worse after the first few days;
- the area feeling hot to touch;
- yellow or green discharge, pus, or a bad smell;
- a fever, shivering, or feeling unwell;
- jewellery that seems to be sinking into the skin, or skin growing over it;
- a lump or sore that is not settling.

Go to A&E or an urgent treatment centre, or call 999, if you have difficulty breathing or swallowing, your face, lips, tongue or throat are swelling (this is especially important after a mouth piercing), you feel faint or collapse, or you feel seriously unwell. Do not wait to contact us first.

If a medical professional asks you to remove jewellery, follow their advice. Please let us know afterwards at thedesk@rebelliousink.tattoo or on [studio phone], so that we can record it and help with the aftercare. We offer a free check-in if you would like us to look at your piercing.
