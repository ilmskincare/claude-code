## 1. What this document is for

Rebellious Ink is a tattoo and piercing studio in Birmingham. Before anyone tattoos or pierces at the premises, the council must have registered both the premises and each person who carries out treatments. This document lists what to prepare, who must be registered, what it costs, and how to behave at an inspection. The cover letter is in section 6.

The legal basis is Part VIII of the Local Government (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act 1982. Birmingham City Council states that it has no power to refuse registration, but the work is controlled through byelaws, and the certificate and a copy of the byelaws must be displayed at the premises. Research sources are in `00-research-notes.md`.

## 2. Who must be registered

The council registers the premises and each person who performs treatment. Its online form accepts one practitioner and one treatment type at a time. A separate application and fee is needed for every practitioner and every treatment type.

## 5. Apprentices

Sophie and Honey are supervised by Elle Evaa. The council page says the person performing the treatment must be registered. The studio's position is that an apprentice does not touch a client with a needle until their own registration is in place, and always works with Elle Evaa on the premises. Until registered, apprentices may observe, set up and clean down, prepare stencils and work on practice materials. The studio will confirm this approach with the council in writing and file the reply.

## 6. How inspections work

The council has no power to refuse registration but inspects against the byelaws, and breaches can lead to prosecution, with fines and suspension or cancellation of the registration. Birmingham's own inspection guidance was not available when this pack was prepared, so the list below is based on the model byelaws and the CIEH tattooing and body piercing guidance toolkit, and the studio should treat it as an expectation, not a promise.

Expect an environmental health or licensing officer, possibly without much notice. Charlie or Elle Evaa should be available during opening hours. Be courteous, show the officer round, answer plainly, and write down any advice given. If the officer finds a fault, agree a date to fix it and record it.

### What the officer will want to see on the day

Registration certificates for premises and each practitioner, displayed, with a copy of the byelaws displayed. No Smoking and No Eating or Drinking notices in the treatment area.

A treatment area used only for treatment, with a smooth impervious floor, smooth impervious couch and work surfaces in good repair, and disposable paper covering changed for every client.

A hand-wash basin with constant hot and cold water, liquid soap and paper towels, for the sole use of operators.

Gloves, aprons, sterile single-use needles and cartridges in date, ink and containers handled as in document 04, and ink batch records.

Sharps bins correctly assembled and not overfilled, a clinical waste contractor agreement, and consignment notes (document 05).

Cleaning schedules completed and signed (document 03), the spillage kit, the premises and equipment register (document 11), and the autoclave logbook and validation and service certificates if any reusable item is sterilised on site.

Signed consent forms and ID records for recent clients, stored securely (document 06), aftercare sheets (document 07).

Training records, hepatitis B status records for operators, supervision records for apprentices (document 08).

The risk assessment, COSHH assessments, accident book and first aid kit (document 09), the complaints and incident log (document 10).

This pack, with each document signed and dated.
