## 1. Purpose

This procedure sets out how Rebellious Ink listens to complaints, records and follows up adverse reactions, and reports serious incidents to the right authority. Anything that goes wrong is treated as a chance to learn, and clients are told plainly that we want to hear.

## 2. Complaints

### How a client can complain

A client can complain in person to any member of staff, by email to thedesk@rebelliousink.tattoo, by telephone on [studio phone], or by post to [premises address]. We display a notice in the waiting area saying how to complain, and the same wording is on the website, rebelliousink.tattoo. Anyone can complain, including a parent, a friend, or a member of the public. A complaint can be made at any time, but is easiest to look into if raised within [six months].

### What we do

1. The first person to hear the complaint listens, writes down the main points on the complaints log, thanks the client, and gives the name of the person who will deal with it. They do not argue or make promises.
2. Elle Evaa acknowledges the complaint within [two working days]. If the complaint is about Elle Evaa, Charlie deals with it.
3. The manager looks into it: speaks to the staff involved, checks the client record, consent form, batch details and cleaning logs, and may ask to see the tattoo or piercing in person.
4. We send a written answer within [ten working days], saying what we found, what we will do, and what the client can do next. If it takes longer, we say why and when they will hear from us.
5. If the answer does not satisfy the client, Charlie reviews the decision within [ten working days] and replies in writing. The client may also contact Trading Standards through the Citizens Advice consumer service, or the Birmingham City Council licensing team at licensing@birmingham.gov.uk if the concern is about hygiene or safety. We will not discourage this.
6. We record the outcome, any refund, repeat work or other remedy, and any change made to our procedures.

A complaint is never held against a client. We keep complaint records for seven years, in line with document 06, and treat them as confidential.

## 3. Adverse reactions

An adverse reaction is anything that goes wrong for a client during or after treatment. It includes an allergic reaction; suspected infection; unusual swelling, bleeding or scarring; fainting; a piercing that is rejected or migrates; unexpected pain; and anything the client or a health professional links to the treatment.

### Recording

The person who hears of it completes the adverse reaction record on the day. It records: date and time; the client's name and contact details; date and type of treatment; the artist or piercer; what happened and when it began, in the client's own words; what advice was given; whether the client has seen a GP, pharmacist, NHS 111 or hospital; the batch details of ink or jewellery used; the cleaning and equipment records for the day; and any photographs the client agrees to. We do not write a diagnosis. We write what the client reports and what we saw, and use "client reports" and "appears" rather than naming a condition.

### Immediate response

Staff follow document 07. Where the client may need urgent help, we tell them to contact their GP or NHS 111, or to go to A&E, and to call 999 in an emergency. We do not give medical advice, and we do not tell a client to stop or start a medicine. We offer to look at the area when they are ready, and to give a written note of what treatment and products were used if their clinician asks for it, with the client's permission.

### Follow-up

Elle Evaa contacts the client within [two working days] to check how they are, and again at [one week] until the problem is resolved or the client chooses to stop. The manager reviews the treatment, products, batch, equipment, cleaning and aftercare for the day, and checks whether other clients treated with the same batch or at the same time have reported problems. If a batch of ink or jewellery may be the cause, it is quarantined and the supplier is told. The result and any change in practice are recorded. Where a client asks for a refund, repeat work or a remedy, the manager considers it fairly and in line with consumer law.

### Learning

Elle Evaa reviews all adverse reactions every three months, looks for patterns, and tells the team. Any change to this pack is recorded in the version history.

## 4. When to tell the council, UKHSA or the HSE

| Event | Who to tell | When |
|---|---|---|
| A suspected cluster of infections linked to the studio, or a client or staff member with a confirmed blood-borne virus believed to be linked to treatment here | Birmingham City Council licensing team (licensing@birmingham.gov.uk) and the UK Health Security Agency local health protection team. The council can give the current contact. | As soon as possible, the same day, and before any public comment. Stop the treatment or equipment involved. |
| A serious adverse reaction or injury to a client needing hospital treatment, believed to be linked to the studio | Birmingham City Council licensing team | Within [one working day]. Also consider a RIDDOR report to the HSE if the person was injured at the premises and taken to hospital. |
| A sharps injury involving known infected blood or body fluid, a worker injury with more than seven days' absence, a specified injury, or an occupational disease | The HSE under RIDDOR, online at https://www.hse.gov.uk/riddor/ | Within the deadline on the HSE site; over-seven-day injuries within 15 days. |
| A breach of the byelaws found by the studio, or a failure of the autoclave leading to uncertainty about sterility of items used on clients | Birmingham City Council licensing team | As soon as possible. Withdraw affected items and trace clients. |
| A change of premises, practitioner or business | Birmingham City Council licensing team | Before the change. A practitioner who moves premises needs a new registration. |
| Loss or unauthorised access to client records | Information Commissioner's Office, and clients affected | Within 72 hours of becoming aware, where required. |
| A crime, violence or threat | Police on 999 or 101 | At once for emergencies. |
| A suspected under-18 attempting to be tattooed or pierced against policy, or evidence of exploitation or abuse of a young person | Police, or the Birmingham children's safeguarding contact [confirm current number with the council] | If a child is at risk, at once. |

The UKHSA health protection team has an out-of-hours service. [Add current local telephone number from the council or UKHSA.]

If in doubt, the manager phones the council licensing team and asks. Telling the council early is always better than later.

## 5. Incident reporting for staff

Staff report any incident or near miss to Elle Evaa the same day, using the incident log. Near misses include a needle left on a tray, a pack found out of date, a cleaning task missed, a tear in a couch, or a client who nearly fainted. Reports are used to improve, and no one is blamed for reporting honestly. Serious incidents, such as sharps injury, serious reaction, violence or fire, are reported to Elle Evaa at once and to Charlie by telephone.
