## 1. Purpose and scope

This policy sets out how everyone working at Rebellious Ink, [premises address], prevents infection between clients, and between clients and staff. It applies to every tattoo artist, apprentice, piercer, receptionist and visitor who enters the treatment area. It follows the byelaws made under Part VIII of the Local Government (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act 1982 and the CIEH tattooing and body piercing guidance toolkit.

Elle Evaa, as studio manager, is the infection control lead. She checks that this policy is followed, and Charlie holds final responsibility.

We treat every client's blood and body fluids as potentially infectious. This is called standard precautions, and it means we do the same thing for every client, whatever we know or do not know about them.

## 2. Hand hygiene

Hands are the most common route for spreading infection, so this is the first rule.

Fingernails are kept short and clean, with no nail extensions or varnish for operators, and no watches, bracelets or rings on the hands or wrists in the treatment area. Any cut, graze or open skin condition on an exposed part of the body is covered with a waterproof dressing. An operator with weeping or broken skin on the hands does not treat clients until it has healed or a manager has agreed an alternative.

Operators wash their hands with liquid soap and warm running water, then dry with disposable paper towels, at these moments: on arriving at work; before setting up for a client; before putting on gloves; after removing gloves; after touching anything that may be contaminated; after using the toilet; after eating, drinking or smoking; and at the end of each treatment. Alcohol hand rub is used between washes only when hands are visibly clean. It does not replace washing.

Hand-wash basins are used for hands only. Nothing else is washed in them. Soap is in wall-mounted dispensers, never refilled by topping up, and paper towels are kept in a dispenser. Posters showing the hand-washing steps are fixed above each basin.

## 3. Gloves and personal protective equipment

Operators wear new disposable nitrile gloves for each client. Gloves are put on after hand washing, once the work area is set up. They are changed whenever they are torn, contaminated, or the operator touches something that is not part of the treatment (a phone, a door handle, a bottle that is not covered). Gloves are changed and hands washed between tasks and when moving from one body area to another where there is any risk of cross-contamination. Gloves are never washed and reused. Latex-free gloves are used as standard so that latex allergy is not a risk to clients.

Operators also wear a clean apron or washable or disposable protective clothing, changed between clients if there is visible contamination and washed at 60 degrees Celsius or hotter at the end of each day if cloth. Eye protection or a visor is worn when there is any risk of splashing, for example when cleaning equipment or dealing with a spill. A surgical mask is worn when the operator has a cough or cold, and during cleaning that creates splashes.

Used gloves and aprons go into the clinical waste bin in the treatment area (document 05). Nobody leaves the treatment area wearing contaminated gloves.

## 4. Skin preparation

The area to be tattooed or pierced is checked for broken skin, rashes, sunburn, infection, moles or other lesions. Treatment does not go ahead on skin that is broken, inflamed or looks infected. We do not diagnose. We tell the client we cannot work on that area today and suggest they speak to their GP or a pharmacist if they are concerned.

The skin is cleaned with a sterile single-use antiseptic wipe or sachet, such as [named product, for example a 2 per cent chlorhexidine in 70 per cent isopropyl alcohol wipe, to be confirmed against the product data sheet], and allowed to dry before the stencil is applied or the needle is used. Where hair needs removing, it is removed with a new disposable razor, which is put straight into the sharps bin. Stencil solution, lubricant and ointment are dispensed from squeezy bottles, tubs and tubes in single-use portions or with a clean spatula, and never by dipping gloved fingers into a shared pot.

For piercing, the skin is cleaned and marked with a single-use marker or a new sterile pen that does not go back into a shared pot. Clamps and forceps are single-use and sterile, or are reprocessed as set out in document 04.

## 5. Single-use sterile equipment

Needles, cartridges, tubes, grips where disposable, razors, piercing needles, receiving tubes, forceps where disposable and ink caps are single-use. They come in sealed sterile packaging, are opened in front of the client, are used for one client only, and are put in a sharps bin straight after use. Anything that has been opened and not used is also thrown away. Packaging that is damaged, wet or past its expiry date is not used. Document 04 sets out the full rules and what happens if the studio ever uses reusable items.

Machines and power supplies are never allowed to touch the client's skin. Machine bodies, clip cords, foot pedals, power units and spray bottles are covered with single-use barrier film or covers, which are changed after each client.

## 6. Preventing cross-contamination

A treatment area is set up fresh for every client. The set-up is done on a clean surface wiped with a hospital grade disinfectant, covered with a clean disposable paper sheet or tray cover.

All items needed for the treatment are put out before gloves are put on, and anything missing is fetched by someone else or after gloves are removed. If an operator needs to open a drawer or touch a bottle with gloved hands, the item is covered or the gloves are changed.

One client at a time is treated at each station. Clients and anyone accompanying them sit outside the sterile work area. Ink is dispensed into a new single-use cap for each client. Ink bottles are wiped clean and kept away from the working area. Bottle tips do not touch ink caps or gloves.

Pets and other animals are not allowed in the premises. The only exception is a recognised assistance dog, which stays in the waiting area and out of the treatment area.

## 7. Cleaning between clients

After each client, and before the next set-up, the operator removes gloves and washes hands, then puts on fresh gloves to clear the station. Waste goes into the correct bin. The couch or chair, work surfaces, arm rests, lamp handles, stool, machine stand and any surface that may have been touched or splashed are cleaned with detergent and then disinfected with a product that is effective against viruses and bacteria, used at the correct dilution and contact time as on the label. Barrier film and paper covers are thrown away. Gloves are removed, hands washed again, and the station is left ready, or set up when the next client is due.

Document 03 has the full cleaning schedule.

## 8. Blood and body fluid spills

A spillage kit is kept in the treatment area and checked monthly (document 11). It contains disposable gloves and aprons, absorbent granules or paper towels, chlorine-releasing granules or a validated alternative, detergent, a scoop and scraper that are disposable, and clinical waste bags.

For a small spill: put on gloves and apron; cover the spill with absorbent material; pour or sprinkle the disinfectant on and leave it for the contact time on the label; scoop up the waste with a disposable scraper into a clinical waste bag; clean the area with detergent and water; disinfect again; dispose of all items as clinical waste; remove gloves and wash hands. Chlorine-releasing products are not used on bleach-sensitive surfaces without checking, and are never mixed with other chemicals. Staff follow the product instructions and the COSHH assessment in document 09.

For a large spill, or one on carpet or fabric, stop treatment, tell Elle Evaa or the person in charge, keep clients away, and follow the product instructions. Carpet and fabric that cannot be disinfected are removed or specially treated.

## 9. Sharps injury or blood and body fluid exposure

A sharps injury means a needle-stick or cut from a used needle or sharp, or blood or body fluid splashing onto broken skin, the eyes, nose or mouth. It can happen to a staff member or to a client.

The first aid steps for a person who is injured are:

1. Encourage the wound to bleed gently. Do not squeeze hard and do not suck it.
2. Wash the wound thoroughly with mild liquid soap and warm running water. Do not scrub.
3. If blood or fluid has splashed into the eyes, rinse them thoroughly with clean running water or sterile saline. If it has splashed in the mouth, spit it out and rinse with plenty of water. Do not swallow.
4. Cover the wound with a waterproof dressing.
5. Stop work, tell Elle Evaa or the person in charge, and secure the item that caused the injury safely without touching it with bare hands.
6. The injured person should get medical advice straight away, on the same day, from NHS 111, their GP, an urgent treatment centre or an emergency department. Say the injury was from a used needle or sharp in a tattoo or piercing studio. In an emergency, call 999. We do not give medical advice about what treatment is needed. That is for the NHS clinician.
7. Where possible and safe, note what was known about the source (the client, if involved, may agree to speak to a health professional). We do not pressure anyone. Details are not shared beyond what is needed for the clinician.
8. The incident is written up in the accident book and the incident log (documents 09 and 10) the same day. The manager decides whether the incident must be reported to the Health and Safety Executive under RIDDOR. A sharps injury involving known infected blood or body fluid is a dangerous occurrence that must be reported. See document 09.
9. The manager reviews the cause with the person involved and records what changes are made, such as a retraining session or a different sharps bin position.

The studio pays for any private medical consultation reasonably needed after an occupational exposure, and for follow-up appointments, subject to [insurer or occupational health arrangements].

## 10. Hepatitis B vaccination and tetanus

Every operator and apprentice is offered hepatitis B vaccination, paid for by the studio, before they start treating clients. The studio recommends it and asks operators to show their vaccination record and, where tested, their antibody result, and keeps a copy. The CIEH toolkit says antibody levels should be checked one to four months after the primary course, and that a single booster should be offered about five years after the primary course to those at continued risk. Vaccination is arranged through [occupational health provider] or the operator's GP. Anyone who chooses not to be vaccinated signs the declination on the training record in document 08. The studio does not give medical advice. The operator's GP or occupational health service decides what applies to them.

Operators are also encouraged to keep their tetanus vaccination up to date, and tell their GP they work with needles and sharp equipment. A copy of the record is kept in the personnel file, with the individual's agreement.

Health information about staff is kept private, held securely, and used only to meet the studio's duties.

## 11. Illness and fitness to work

An operator who has diarrhoea, vomiting, a fever, a heavy cold, a skin infection on the hands or forearms, or any condition the manager decides could put a client at risk, does not treat clients. They tell Elle Evaa or Charlie before the shift, rest and follow NHS advice, and return when they are well. A client who is unwell on arrival is rebooked without charge, where a clear reason exists, and the studio explains why.

## 12. Monitoring and records

Elle Evaa checks cleaning logs weekly and signs them off. Each month she reviews the spillage kit, sharps bins, hand-wash facilities and supplies. Each quarter she carries out a short self-audit using the audit tool in the CIEH toolkit, [audit date and reviewer], and records any actions. Records are kept on the premises for at least seven years, in line with document 06.

## 13. Training

Everyone reads this policy when they start, signs to confirm they have read and understood it, and repeats this at each annual review. Infection control training is recorded in document 08.
