## 1. Purpose

This document sets the standard the premises must meet and the cleaning that keeps them there. It follows byelaw 2 of the model byelaws for tattooing and cosmetic piercing (cleanliness of premises and fittings) and should be checked against Birmingham's adopted byelaws once received. It covers [premises address], with [number] treatment rooms and [number] stations. Opening hours are [hours].

## 2. Premises standards

### Separate treatment area

Tattooing and piercing are done only in a treatment area used solely for that purpose. It is not used for eating, drinking, storage of food, staff rest, or as a thoroughfare to other parts of the building. It is separated from the waiting area by a wall, partition or door. Clients and visitors who are not being treated stay in the waiting area unless the artist has agreed they can sit near, in which case they stay outside the clean work zone.

### Floors, walls and ceilings

The floor of the treatment area has a smooth impervious surface, such as sealed vinyl or sealed hard flooring, with no gaps or cracks, and coved or sealed at the edges where practical. No carpet is used in treatment areas. Walls, doors, partitions and ceilings are kept clean and in good repair, painted with a washable finish or tiled, so they can be cleaned effectively.

### Surfaces and furniture

Every table, couch, chair or stool in the treatment area that may be contaminated with blood or other body fluids has a smooth impervious surface, with no tears or splits. Any torn upholstery is repaired or the item replaced straight away. Any surface on which a needle, instrument or equipment is placed before treatment is also smooth and impervious. Couches and chairs are covered with a clean disposable paper sheet that is changed for each client.

### Hand-wash basins

There is at least one hand-wash basin in or next to the treatment area [number and location]. It is used only for hand washing by operators. It has a constant supply of clean hot and cold water, ideally from a mixer tap that is not hand-operated, liquid soap in a wall dispenser, disposable paper towels in a dispenser, and a foot-operated waste bin. There is no plug, no nail brush and nothing stored in the basin. A separate sink is provided for cleaning equipment, [location], and is clearly labelled. Hand hygiene posters are displayed above the basin.

### Sanitary accommodation

Suitable sanitary accommodation for operators is available at [location]. It has hot and cold water, soap and paper towels, is cleaned daily, and is not used for storing clinical items.

### Lighting

The treatment area has bright, even lighting at the work surface so the artist can see the skin and work clearly, with [type of light, for example task lamp with cleanable shade]. Lamp arms and handles are wiped between clients. Any bulbs are shielded or contained so that broken glass cannot fall into the work area. Lighting levels are [checked against the studio risk assessment in document 09].

### Ventilation

The premises are ventilated by [natural or mechanical ventilation, for example extractor fan and opening windows] so that air in the treatment area is fresh and fumes from disinfectants and cleaning products do not build up. Extract fans are cleaned and maintained as per [schedule]. Temperature is kept comfortable at about [temperature] for clients and staff.

### Utilities

There are enough safe gas points (if used) and electrical sockets for the equipment. Electrical equipment is tested as set out in document 09, and extension leads are not daisy-chained.

### Storage

There is clean, dry and suitable storage for sterile items, ink, jewellery and consumables, away from sinks, bins and the floor. Sterile packs are kept in closed cupboards or drawers off the floor and are not stored in the treatment area where they could be splashed. Stock is rotated by expiry date. Cleaning products and chemicals are stored separately in a locked or controlled place, with safety data sheets (document 09).

### Animals, smoking, eating and drinking

No animals are permitted on the premises other than a recognised assistance dog, which stays out of the treatment area. No smoking or vaping is allowed anywhere inside the premises. No eating or drinking is allowed in the treatment area by anyone, and staff eat and drink only in [staff area]. Clients may be offered water in the waiting area only. "No Smoking" and "No Eating or Drinking" notices are prominently displayed in the treatment area, [location of notices].

### Waiting area

The waiting area is clean, uncluttered and kept in good repair, with seating that can be wiped. It has no sharps, clinical waste or ink. Portfolios and books are kept in wipeable covers. Children and young people waiting for a parent or guardian, or accompanying them, must be supervised by an adult and kept out of the treatment area.

## 4. Cleaning schedule

The person who completes each task initials the log (section 5). Elle Evaa signs off each week.

### Between every client (treatment area)

Dispose of all single-use items, sharps and waste correctly. Wipe the couch or chair, arm rests, stool, machine stand, lamp handles, tray, work surfaces and any other touched surfaces with detergent, then disinfect and allow the contact time. Replace the paper sheet, tray cover and barrier film. Check the sharps bin is not above the fill line. Wash hands and set up for the next client. Wipe or replace pens, stencil printer handle and any shared item.

### Daily: opening

Check the premises for damage or anything unusual overnight. Open windows or turn on ventilation. Check the first aid kit, spillage kit, soap and paper towel supplies, and fill dispensers. Check the hand-wash basin is working with hot and cold water. Check the autoclave and ultrasonic cleaner, if used, and complete the daily test (document 04). Wipe all surfaces in the treatment area with disinfectant before the first client.

### Daily: closing

Disinfect all work surfaces, couches, chairs and stools in the treatment area at the end of the working day (as the byelaws require). Wash the floor of the treatment area with detergent, then disinfect. Empty the clinical waste bin if full and replace the liner, and take clinical waste to the storage area. Empty general waste. Clean and disinfect the hand-wash basin, sink, taps and sanitary accommodation. Clean the waiting area surfaces, door handles and counters. Empty the sink and leave dry. Check the sharps bins. Turn off equipment and lock cupboards. Complete the closing checklist in document 11.

### Weekly

Wash the treatment area walls and doors where splashed, and clean around the edges of the floor. Clean light fittings and lamp shades. Clean inside drawers and cupboards in the treatment area and wipe the outside of storage cupboards. Clean fridge if used for ink, aftercare or sterile items, and check the temperature log. Clean the sink and tap bases and remove limescale. Clean mirrors, windows, waiting area furniture and floors. Wash cloth towels, gowns and covers. Check and restock all supplies. Check the sharps bin collection date and clinical waste store.

### Monthly

Deep clean the treatment area including behind equipment, under couches and the skirting. Descale taps and shower heads where fitted, and run taps for several minutes if rarely used. Clean ventilation grilles and extractor fans. Check upholstery for splits and replace as needed. Check the spillage kit, first aid kit, fire extinguishers and fire alarm test log. Wash curtains or blinds. Clean the waiting area thoroughly including behind seating. Review the cleaning log for gaps and agree actions. Check expiry dates on all sterile stock, antiseptics and disinfectants.

### Quarterly and annually

Quarterly, Elle Evaa carries out the self-audit from the CIEH toolkit. Annually, premises are redecorated or touched up where paint is chipped, flooring is checked, the autoclave service, validation and electrical testing are in date, and this document is reviewed.

## 6. When standards are not met

If a fault is found, such as a torn couch, broken basin tap or flood, the station or area is taken out of use until it is fixed. The fault is written in the incident log (document 10) with the date repaired. Clients booked for that station are moved to another or rebooked. The manager does not leave the premises with an unresolved fault that could put a client at risk.
