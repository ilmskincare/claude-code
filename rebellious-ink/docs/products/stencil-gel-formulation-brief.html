Stencil transfer gel: formulation brief

1. Design rationale

Commercial transfer gels are humectant solutions (propylene glycol, glycerin) that wet the thermal stencil ink and hold it, with alcohol for flash-off and a gel thickener. Humectants alone stay re-wettable, so the stencil smears under green soap. This brief adds a film former (PVP, or PVP/VA for water resistance) so that once the alcohol flashes off, the stencil ink is locked in a dry flexible film. Glycerin is kept deliberately low to limit smear. No fragrance, because the skin is about to be broken.

3. Method of manufacture, pilot 1 kg

  1. Phase A: heat water to 40°C. Sprinkle hydroxyethylcellulose under high shear. Hydrate with intermittent stirring 20 to 30 minutes until clear and lump-free.
  2. Add PVP to Phase A slowly under stirring until fully dissolved.
  3. Phase B: add propylene glycol, then glycerin. Stir to uniform.
  4. Cool to below 30°C. Phase C: add isopropyl alcohol under gentle stirring with the vessel covered to limit loss.
  5. Phase D: add preservative and colour. Stir 10 minutes. Check pH, viscosity and appearance against section 5.
  6. Fill into 250 ml PET squeeze bottles with flip-top caps. Batch code and PAO on the label.

Note for the lab: at 15% IPA, confirm flash point for storage, transport and labelling classification. The product should be stored away from heat and ignition sources either way.

6. Performance test (decides which variant is approved)

StepDetail
1Same drawing, same thermal stencil paper, same printer settings for every sample. Practice skin, cleaned with IPA and fully dry.
2Thin even layer of gel, stencil pressed for 15 seconds, peeled. Record time to dry-to-touch.
3At 15 minutes, begin wiping: folded paper towel wetted with 1:8 green soap solution, firm strokes across the lines, same hand, same pressure.
4Score line integrity 1 to 5 at 10, 25, 50 and 100 wipes (5 = untouched, 3 = faded but usable, 1 = gone).
5Repeat each sample with the studio's own glide applied to the skin before stencilling, because glide is the usual cause of lift.
6Benchmarks run alongside: Hornet Stencil and one other commercial transfer gel.
7Pass mark: score 4 or better at 100 wipes, with glide. Then a real session on a consenting artist's own skin, after the safety assessment is in place, before any client.

7. Regulatory and PIF checklist

  • Responsible Person: iLM (existing). Product category: skin care, leave-on, cosmetic. Not a medical device, no medicinal claims.
  • Cosmetic Product Safety Report from the qualified safety assessor, covering the PVP and glycol levels and the alcohol content on intact skin. Stencil gel is applied before the skin is broken; state this use clearly so the assessor does not treat it as a wound product.
  • Challenge test ISO 11930, stability per section 5, microbiology on each batch.
  • UK SCPN notification before first use on any client, including in the studio.
  • Manufacture under ISO 22716 GMP with batch records retained.
  • Label: product name, function, 250 ml e, INCI list in descending order, PAO 12M symbol, batch code, RP name and UK address, warnings: external use only, avoid eyes, keep away from children, flammable if classification requires it.
  • Draft INCI for the label (base formula): Aqua, Propylene Glycol, Isopropyl Alcohol, Glycerin, PVP, Hydroxyethylcellulose, Phenoxyethanol, Ethylhexylglycerin, CI 19140, CI 16035
  • Claims to substantiate: "stencil stays put through long sessions" needs the wipe-test data on file. Avoid "medical", "surgical", "sterile", "antibacterial".

8. Directions for the label

Shave and clean the area, then dry it completely. Apply a thin, even layer of gel. Place the stencil, press firmly for 15 seconds, peel. Do not touch for 10 to 15 minutes while it sets. Blot, never rub. Work from the bottom up and keep alcohol wipes away from the stencil. External use only. Avoid contact with eyes; rinse with water if it occurs. Keep out of reach of children.
